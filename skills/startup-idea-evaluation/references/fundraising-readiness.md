# Fundraising Readiness — checklist, dilution math, pitch structure

Three tools: (1) a readiness checklist with 2026 metric bars by round, (2) a worked SAFE dilution example so you know what a raise actually costs, (3) the canonical pitch structure. A raise is a **designed, parallel, compressed process** — not a series of coffee chats — and the strongest negotiating position is not needing the money. [Evergreen]

---

## Part 1: Readiness by round — metrics and milestones [2026]

The rungs are [Evergreen]; every number below is [2026] and drifts yearly — re-verify against current Carta data before raising.

### Pre-seed (~$500K–$2.5M on SAFEs)
- [ ] No revenue required — you're selling team + insight + a prototype. Strong technical AI founders raise on reputation alone.
- [ ] Median post-money SAFE caps step with round size: ~$8M (sub-$250K rounds) → ~$10M ($250–499K) → ~$12M ($500–999K) → ~$15M ($1M–2.4M). 92% of pre-seed rounds use SAFEs.
- [ ] Typical dilution: ~12.5% median.
- [ ] YC's standard deal (if that path): $500K total = $125K for 7% + $375K on an uncapped MFN SAFE.

### Seed (~$3–4M; ~$20M post-money median, heavily AI-skewed)
- [ ] "Seed is the new Series A": many seed funds want **$300–500K ARR** or steep usage growth before leading. Pre-revenue seeds still happen for repeat founders and hot AI theses.
- [ ] Evidence of pull beats any metric: waitlist growth, week-over-week usage, unsolicited inbound, design partners.
- [ ] Non-AI seeds track ~$18M pre-money on ~$3M raised; AI-application seeds $4–5M on $20–22M post. AI takes ~42%+ of all seed capital.
- [ ] Typical dilution: 15–20% to investors + option pool → total founder dilution commonly 20–28%. Median founder-team ownership after seed: ~56%.
- [ ] Plan the seed to last: only ~20–25% of recent seed cohorts graduate to an A, and median seed→A time is 2.1–2.2 years. Size runway 24–36 months.

### Series A (the real gate)
- [ ] ARR: **$1–2M floor; realistically $2M+ for non-AI**; $3–5M median for hot AI deals, with 2–3x YoY (10–15%+ MoM) growth.
- [ ] Quality bars: NRR >110% (AI often >120%), GRR ≥85–90%, gross margin >60% AI-native / 75%+ pure SaaS, CAC payback <12–18 months, burn multiple <2 (ideally <1.5), 12+ months runway remaining at raise.
- [ ] Investors read metrics in a fixed order: **size (ARR) → growth → quality (retention, margin) → efficiency (payback, burn multiple)**. Know each cold — hesitating on your own churn number is worse than the number. [Evergreen]

### Pre-raise process checklist [Evergreen]
- [ ] Deck and/or memo, simple financial model, data room (incorporation docs, cap table, metrics) ready *before* any investor conversation — you get one shot per investor per year.
- [ ] Tracked list of 40–80 investors who actually invest at your stage/sector/check size, ranked by fit; warm-intro path identified for each (warm intros convert ~10x better than cold).
- [ ] All first meetings compressed into a 2–3 week parallel window — serial conversations let investors stall you; the first committed check is half the battle, and investors herd.
- [ ] Announce the *low* end of your range ("raising $2M," oversubscribe to $3M gracefully).
- [ ] Raise sized to a milestone, not a vibe: what does it cost to reach the metrics the *next* round requires, +25% buffer? Fully-loaded headcount: ~$20–30K/person/month for senior NYC/SF engineers.
- [ ] Know default alive vs. default dead before walking in: at current growth and burn, do you reach profitability before cash-out? Half of founders can't answer; the calculation takes five minutes.
- [ ] Red flags to refuse at seed: investor board *control*, >1x or participating liquidation preference, anyone asking >25%.
- [ ] Signaling-risk check: keep multi-stage-fund seed checks small relative to the round — if the famous fund that knows you best doesn't lead your A, everyone else asks why.
- [ ] "It's not a deal till the money's in the bank" — keep parallel conversations warm until close.

## Part 2: SAFE dilution — the worked example

The SAFE (Simple Agreement for Future Equity): money now, shares later at your next priced round, converting at a **valuation cap** (effectively the price of the deal). Post-money SAFEs (the standard since 2018) lock in the investor's exact percentage — which produces the founder-side gotcha: **post-money SAFEs don't dilute each other; every SAFE you stack comes entirely out of the founders and common stockholders.** [Evergreen mechanics, 2026-typical numbers]

You and a co-founder own 100% (50/50):

1. **Pre-seed SAFE #1:** $500K at a $5M post-money cap → investor locked at **10%** ($500K ÷ $5M).
2. **Pre-seed SAFE #2** (a year later): $1M at a $10M post-money cap → locked at **10%**.
3. Immediately before the priced seed: SAFE holders 20%, founders **80%** (40% each). Both SAFEs' dilution came entirely out of you.
4. **Priced seed:** $4M at $16M pre-money = $20M post-money → new investors get $4M ÷ $20M = **20%**. Term sheet also requires a **10% post-round option pool** (created pre-money — i.e., out of you, not the investors: the classic first-timer blind spot where 8–12% of founder equity quietly vanishes).
5. New investors (20%) + pool (10%) = 30% new. Everyone pre-existing is squeezed into the remaining 70%:
   - Founders: 80% × 0.70 = **56%** (28% each)
   - SAFE #1: 10% × 0.70 = 7%; SAFE #2: 7%
   - New investors 20% + pool 10% = 100% ✓

That 56% lands almost exactly on Carta's real-world median (~56.2% post-seed; ~36% post-A; ~23% post-B — roughly 20 points per early round). This is the *normal* path. The horror story is stacking $3M of SAFEs at a $6M cap out of desperation: 50% gone before the seed even starts, and a cap table that makes Series A investors walk.

**Rules:**
- [ ] Track **fully-diluted ownership** from day one — your percentage as if every SAFE converted at its cap, the whole option pool existed, and all warrants exercised. That number, not issued shares, is your real ownership.
- [ ] Negotiate the option pool against your actual 18-month hiring plan (median grants: first hire 1.5%, second 0.85%, third 0.5% — a 10% pool covers a lot). A demanded 15% pool is usually price negotiation wearing a costume.
- [ ] Dilution compounds — each round multiplies against what's left — but don't over-optimize it: Graham's equity equation says selling n% is worth it whenever the trade makes the company more than 1/(1−n) times more valuable. Owning 30% of $1B beats 60% of $50M.
- [ ] Grant pro-rata rights only to investors you'd happily have double down; they consume space in your next round.

## Part 3: The pitch structure

The pitch is a compression test of your thinking, not a slide performance. Investors are scoring: do I understand it, am I excited, do I like the team — plus clarity of thought, rate of learning, and market pull. [Evergreen]

### The 10-slide skeleton (merged Sequoia/YC)

| # | Slide | Must prove |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | One-liner | Understood in one sentence, in your mom's vocabulary — name the thing, the problem, the customer |
| 2 | Problem | Severe, frequent, quantified pain with a budget owner — told through one real user's story with numbers |
| 3 | Solution | 10x, not 10%; start from the problem, not the technology |
| 4 | Why now | A recent change that answers "why hasn't this been built already?" |
| 5 | Market | Venture-scale prize from **bottom-up math only** (customers × price); never "1% of a Gartner number" |
| 6 | Product | It exists and works — demo > screenshots > mockups > words |
| 7 | Traction | The market is pulling: growth *rate* beats absolute numbers at seed; real axes |
| 8 | Competition + insight | You know the landscape (never "no competition" — name the status quo and spreadsheets) and understand something others don't |
| 9 | Team | Founder-market fit stories, not logos-and-titles résumés |
| 10 | Ask | "$2M to reach $1M ARR in 18 months" — amount, milestones, runway, and what the next round requires |

### The three zoom levels [Evergreen]
- [ ] **30 seconds** (three sentences): what you do in plain words → size of the pain/market → traction or unfair advantage.
- [ ] **2 minutes:** the above + your insight + the demo moment + team one-liner + the ask.
- [ ] **20 minutes + Q&A:** the full deck, story compressed to 20 minutes so the hour has room for questions. If the 60-second version doesn't work, the 20-minute version won't either.

### The narrative arc (Raskin) [Evergreen]
Status quo → a big change in the world (your why-now) → winners and losers → the promised land → your product as the vehicle → proof it's already happening.

### 2026-specific reflexes [2026]
- [ ] **Demo-first:** show the magic in 60 seconds at the *start* — but pair it with reliability evidence (task success rates, eval results, retention), because VCs are burned by wrappers that demoed well and churned.
- [ ] **Margins interrogation:** have a worked answer to "what's your gross margin and how does it improve?" — e.g., "each $500/mo customer costs ~$120/mo inference (76% GM); caching + routing cuts it to ~$60 by year-end (88%)." "Models will get cheaper" alone = pass.
- [ ] **The wrapper question:** a prepared answer to "what happens when OpenAI ships this?"
- [ ] **Sell outcomes, not assistants:** "this work costs $X/year, recurs, and has a named budget owner" beats "this workflow is inefficient." Size the *labor* being replaced, not the software category.
- [ ] **Velocity as a team signal:** "two founders, 10 weeks, live product, 4 paying customers" now carries the weight a year of stealth used to.
- [ ] Pre-empt your two worst objections yourself (churn, regulation, incumbent response); "I don't know, but here's how I'd find out" beats a confident wrong answer.

## Output format for a readiness run

Score each checklist item met / missing / unknown; state which round the evidence actually supports (often one rung lower than hoped); compute fully-diluted founder ownership under the proposed raise; and list the 3 highest-leverage gaps to close before the first investor meeting.
